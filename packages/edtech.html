Why the other side of the table matters

Schools don't buy features. They buy trust, timing, and the absence of risk. I know the budget calendar, the board agenda, the privacy questions the tech director will ask, and the difference between a pilot that renews and one that quietly expires in June. That's what I bring to your outreach, your demo, and your booth: not a script, but the judgment of someone who has said no to a hundred vendors and yes to a few.

FAQ

What stage are you built for?

Seed to Series B companies selling to K-12 or early childhood, with a working product and a founder or small team doing the selling. If you already have a ten-person sales org, you need a VP, not me.

Do you replace our sales team?

No. I make the one you have effective in schools. Where there isn't one yet, I run outreach and demos until there is, and I hand off everything I built.

Will you work under our brand?

Yes. Outreach can go out under your name or mine, whichever converts better for your audience. At conferences I'm part of your team.

Can this combine with the school packages?

Often. I work with schools every week, which is where the instincts come from. I'm careful about conflicts: I won't pitch your product into a school I'm currently consulting for.

Tell me where the pipeline stalls.

I'll tell you what the buyer is thinking at that exact moment.
